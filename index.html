Case studies

Streamlining syndrome-tracking in the EHR

Problem

A hospital's clinical team flagged a recurring friction point: tracking specific syndrome patterns inside their EHR meant pulling and reconciling several manual reports, slowing down day-to-day clinical visibility.

Approach

We opened a direct line with the hospital's clinical informatics team to validate the request and see how peer provider hospitals handled similar tracking, then partnered with the EHR development team to assess technical feasibility. Once the build was confirmed viable, we worked through the clinical implications before implementing the configuration and validating it against the existing workflow.

Result

The hospital moved from several disconnected manual reports to one streamlined workflow for syndrome tracking inside their EHR — cutting reporting overhead and giving clinical staff faster visibility into patient patterns.

Finding the root cause behind recurring tickets

Problem

Tickets rarely arrive labeled as symptoms of something bigger — a handful of similar-looking ITSM tickets can read as unrelated one-offs until someone actually looks for the pattern.

Approach

When several tickets point in the same direction, I trace them back to a shared root cause instead of closing each in isolation, then take the fix through peer review so it holds up across the environment rather than patching the one ticket in front of me.

Result

Recurring ticket volume drops, because the underlying issue gets resolved once, properly, instead of being patched repeatedly.

Catching a requirement gap before go-live

Problem

A configuration change can look release-ready on paper and still miss a specific business requirement that only surfaces once it's actually run against real workflows.

Approach

I run structured, workflow-level UAT before anything ships — walking each change through the business requirement it's meant to satisfy, not just a technical checklist — and flag mismatches before they reach production.

Result

Gaps get caught and corrected pre-release, protecting end users from disruptions that would otherwise only surface after go-live.

Keeping a cross-environment migration clean

Problem

Moving a batch of build and configuration changes across environments is where small inconsistencies quietly creep in — one missed step in one environment can undo hours of work in another.

Approach

I document each migration in Excel as it happens, using AI tools to speed up the reporting, so every change is traceable across environments and nothing gets left behind or duplicated.

Result

Migrations land clean, with a documented trail that makes any later audit or rollback straightforward.

Aligning clinical and technical stakeholders on one build

Problem

Clinical and technical stakeholders often want different things from the same system change — clinical teams focused on patient workflow, technical teams focused on what's actually feasible to build.

Approach

I sit between both sides, translating clinical intent into technical scope and technical constraints back into terms clinical stakeholders can act on, until both groups are signing off on the same shared understanding.

Result

The final build reflects what clinical teams actually need, without asking the technical team to build something unworkable.
